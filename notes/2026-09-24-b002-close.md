# b002 close: two finalists, 3.4x and 7.5x smaller; close on tracking and recovery, not on falls

Batch `b002-student-size-longer` ran to its pre-registered end in 66.5 min on an RTX 3050
Laptop (4 GB). Two students reached the final rung and passed every final keep line there:
a01 (256-128-64) and a02 (128-128). The record's `duckbatch_commit` reads `2da6dfc`; the batch
started at `42fe3d1`. `2da6dfc` only added b001's Jev replay JSON, so the code is identical. The
runner now reads the commit at start.

## Final: held-out seeds 2001-2003, 30 s walk + 6 s recovery each, teacher in the same runs

| | Teacher | a01 256-128-64 | a02 128-128 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parameters | 197,774 | 57,934 (3.4x smaller) | 26,254 (7.5x smaller) |
| Planar error, m/s | 0.174 ± 0.004 | 0.178 ± 0.004 (1.02x) | 0.182 ± 0.001 (1.05x) |
| Yaw error, rad/s | 0.368 ± 0.003 | 0.388 ± 0.007 (1.06x) | 0.407 ± 0.005 (1.11x) |
| Falls per min (walking) | 0.14 ± 0.02 | 0.38 ± 0.07 | 0.46 ± 0.06 |
| Time down | 0.43% | 0.85% | 1.12% |
| Gets up from prone within 6 s | 96.9% ± 1.5 | 98.2% ± 0.5 | 97.1% ± 0.5 |
| Time to get up | 0.56 s | 0.56 s | 0.56 s |
| p50 latency, 1 thread (laptop) | 29.0 µs | 14.5 µs | 10.8 µs (2.7x faster) |

- **Tracking and getting up:** at the teacher's level to within 2-11%.
- **Falls are not.** Both students fall about 3x as often as the teacher under the stumble
  pushes: once every 2.2 to 2.6 minutes of walking, against once every 7. Every student seed is
  outside the teacher's seed range (teacher max 0.164, student min 0.282). On the held-out
  seeds a02 sits just over the pre-registered falls keep line (+0.32 against +0.25), although
  it passed at rung 2 (seed 1000).
- **My expectation was wrong on both counts.** I expected 128-128 to hold within noise and yaw
  to be the hard part. Yaw came within 11%. Falls are the gap.

## On-device cost

Measured with onnxruntime at batch 1 on one thread, with recorded sim observations
(`bench.json`).

| | Params | p50 fp32 | p50 int8 | int8 max action drift |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Teacher | 197,774 | 29.0 µs | 18.1 µs | 0.17 |
| a01 | 57,934 | 14.5 µs | 20.9 µs | 0.13 |
| a02 | 26,254 | 10.8 µs | 12.3 µs | 0.17 |

Dynamic int8 quantization speeds up only the teacher. On small students it is slower
(per-call quantize overhead), and it moves some joint target by up to 0.13 to 0.17 rad on every
model. For this policy family, a smaller fp32 network beats a quantized big one.

## Decision models (shadow on every case, identical text)

Of the cases the final rules settle, b002 has 3 clear kills (a04, a05, a06 at rung 0) and 2
keeps (the finalists).

| | Agrees with the rules | Confident (≥ 0.90) and wrong | Answer pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| GLiNER2.5-Decide | 3/5 | **2**: `kill` at 0.92 and 0.93 on both finalists | `kill`, gap 2, confidence 0.92-0.94 on all 17 cases in b001 and b002 |
| Jev | 4/5 | 0 | its `gap` tracks quality: 2.75 (worst) down to 1.08 and 0.26 (finalists) |

- Had Decide been allowed to act alone at 0.90, it would have killed both results. On this
  task, rendered this way, it does not read the numbers. This matches base GLiNER2 in rtlab.
  The case text may be off-distribution for a model built for intents, tickets and reviews; a
  fine-tune on labelled cases is the fair next test, not a verdict on the model.
- Jev's `extend`/`kill` choice is weaker than its `gap`. It chose `kill` on a02 at rung 2, but
  at 0.11 confidence, so it would have gone to a person, never to an automatic kill. It also
  said `still_learning` of about 0.17 at rung 0, and the arms did improve with more budget.
  The `gap` score is the channel to build on.
- **The honest summary:** in these batches the rules did all the deciding. The decision
  models' value so far is measurement: one of them would have been dangerous as an actor, and
  the other gives a severity score worth using.
