# b001 design: how small can Pollen's default walker get? (pre-registered 2026-09-24)

Written and committed before `b001-student-size` ran. The close-out goes in
`2026-09-24-b001-close.md`, which will say where this design turned out wrong.

## Question

Pollen's shipped default walker, `velstand.onnx` (61-512-256-128-14, 197,774 parameters), runs
on the robot's RK3566 at 50 Hz. How small can a student distilled from it be before it
measurably walks worse, in the same simulator Pollen trained it in, with domain randomization,
pushes and observation noise left on?

## Method

- **Task:** `Mjlab-VelStand-Flat-MicroDuck` from `pollen-robotics/microduck_rl@cb70b79`,
  curricula forced to their final stage.
- **Teacher:** `pollen-robotics/microduck-policies@1b56c39/velstand.onnx`, loaded into torch
  (max abs difference from onnxruntime 8.6e-6).
- **Students:** six ELU MLPs from 256-128-64 (57,934 params) down to 32-32 (3,502 params). All
  use lr 1e-3 and the teacher's normalizer, so each exports as the same
  `Sub, Div, Gemm...` graph Pollen's tools load.
- **Training:** population DAgger. The 512 envs (the 4 GB ceiling for this task) are split
  between the six students and the teacher, which runs as a reference arm. The teacher labels
  every state. `beta` (the teacher-override probability) decays from 1 to 0 over 40 iterations.
- **Search:** successive halving with eta 2, over rungs of 60, 120 and 240 iterations. A closed
  arm's envs go to the survivors.
- **Judge:** the pre-registered rules in `src/duckbatch/batch/judge.py` (`DEFAULT_GATES`), then
  Jev on the cases the rules cannot call (it acts alone at confidence 0.90 or higher), then a
  person. Survivors are chosen in `efficiency` rank: arms that pass every keep line advance
  smallest-first.
- **Decision models, measured:** every case is rendered to one text (`judge.render_case`). The
  text holds the observed numbers, the ratios to the teacher and the lab conventions, but never
  which rule line was crossed. That same text goes to fastino/GLiNER2.5-Decide (local, open,
  340M, pinned revision `65624f1`) and to Jev, in shadow, for every arm at every rung. The
  cases the rules settle are free labels, so each model's agreement with the rules on those
  cases is the measurement. Decide may not act in b001: it is unmeasured, and base GLiNER2
  missed 52 of 58 numeric-threshold cases in rtlab. Case texts are stored, so a model that was
  not available at run time (Jev, if no key is set) is replayed over the identical cases
  afterwards with `duckbatch rejudge`.
- **Reported numbers:** every finalist is exported to ONNX and evaluated for 30 s on held-out
  seeds 2001, 2002 and 2003, with the teacher in the same eval. The teacher's spread across
  seeds is the noise floor, and any gap smaller than it is reported as no gap.
- **On-device cost:** `duckbatch bench` measures parameters, FLOPs and bytes, plus 1-thread
  onnxruntime latency on this laptop. The latency is a ratio, not the robot's number. It also
  measures an int8 dynamic-quantized copy, with its action drift from fp32 on recorded
  observations.

## Correction before any result (2026-09-24, same day)

The first launch of b001 was stopped during rung 0, before any evaluation had been written or
read. The evaluation was wrong: at its final curriculum stage VelStand spawns 45% of episodes
prone and 20% crouched, and adds "topple" pushes of up to 1.2 m/s to *train* recovery. The fall
counter scored every one of those as a walking fall. Under that mix, Pollen's own teacher read
5.2 falls/min and 14.5% of the time down.

Changes, all made before any b001 number existed:

- **Walk eval** (`sim.EVAL_PROFILES["walk"]`): HOME spawns and the ordinary ±0.3 m/s stumble
  pushes, with no prone or crouch spawns and no topples. The teacher reads 0.18 to 0.19
  falls/min and 0.5% down on seed 2001. The same seed run twice differs by about 3 to 4%
  (MuJoCo-Warp is not bitwise deterministic), which is part of the noise floor.
- **Recovery eval** (`"recover"`): every episode spawns prone (half face-down, half on a side).
  An arm has recovered if it is upright (tilt under 30 degrees) for 0.5 s within 6 s. The
  teacher: 97.7%, mean 0.55 s.
- **Tracking error** is averaged only while upright.
- **New gates:** keep if recovery is at most 10 points below the teacher's; kill if it is more
  than 30 points below. The rank score gains `3 x (teacher recovery - arm recovery)`.
- **Training is unchanged.** Students still learn on the task's full mix, which includes the
  prone spawns and topples, so they learn to get up too.

## Kill order (what closes an attempt)

1. A non-finite metric.
2. Falls more than 1.5 per env-minute above the teacher's, or more than 10% of the time spent
   tilted past 60 degrees.
3. Planar or yaw tracking error more than 1.6x the teacher's (while upright), or a recovery
   rate more than 30 points below the teacher's.
4. Anything between the keep line and the kill line goes to the decision model, then a person.
5. Outranked: more survivors passed than the rung's quota allows.

## Expectation to beat

My guess, written down so it can be wrong: 128-128 (26k params, 7.5x smaller) holds the
teacher's line within noise, 64-64 (8.5k) shows a small tracking gap, and 32-32 falls
measurably more. If 64-64 holds within noise, the robot runs about 23x fewer parameters for
the same walk.

## Expectation to beat, decision models

On the rule-settled cases, I expect Jev to agree with the rules at 80% or better, and Decide at
55 to 70%. Decide is a text classifier reading numbers; the ratios are pre-computed for it,
which is its best chance. On the uncertain cases I expect the two to disagree often, and there
is no ground truth there beyond what the later rungs show.

## Not claimed

- **Sim only.** Nothing here has run on a real Microduck. A student that matches the teacher
  in mjlab is a candidate for a hardware test, not a result about hardware.
- **Distillation is imitation, not RL.** The RL is Pollen's, inside the teacher; this batch
  measures how much of it survives compression. PPO fine-tuning of a student is a later batch.
- **Latency comes from an x86 laptop,** not the RK3566.
