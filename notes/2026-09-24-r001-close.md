# r001 close: both models pass the router gate, and they fail in different ways

The run is `duckbatch route-eval menus/r001-router-requests.yaml`: 46 pre-registered requests
and 55 gold steps, with records in `records/r001-router-requests/{decide,jev}.json`. The gate
was at least 85% action accuracy and at least 5 of 7 out-of-scope requests refused. **Both
pass.**

| | GLiNER2.5-Decide (local, 340M, CPU) | Jev (hosted) |
|---|---|---|
| Action | 47/55 (85.5%) | **55/55 (100%)** |
| Speed (movement clauses) | 23/24 | 24/24 |
| Head (movement clauses) | 19/24 | 18/24 |
| Sound | 8/8 | 8/8 |
| Whole request exactly right | 32/46 | 40/46 |
| Out-of-scope refused | 6/7 | 7/7 |
| Mean confidence when wrong | **0.37** | 0.95 |

## Where each fails

- **Jev: all 6 errors are one ambiguity in my gold labels.** For "turn left", "spin to your
  left quickly" and "rotate clockwise", Jev puts the head in the turn's direction at 0.81 to
  0.99 confidence; my gold says straight. Looking where you turn is a reasonable reading, so
  this is a labelling choice, not clearly a model error. The pre-registered score stands. With
  "head follows the turn" accepted, Jev would be 24/24 on head and 46/46 exact.
- **Decide: most of its action errors trace to my label descriptions.** I described
  `unsupported` as "... or a question, not a command". Decide then called "ask a question",
  "say hello", "chirp" and "wheee" unsupported, each at 0.20 to 0.32 confidence. The other
  errors:
  - "stand still" became `sit_or_stand` (the word "stand"), at 0.41;
  - "pick up the red cup" became `peck_ground`, at 0.32;
  - "walk slowly while looking to the right" became `turn_right`, at 0.26;
  - on head, it made the same turn-direction reading as Jev on 4 clauses, at 0.46 to 0.56.

## Confidence is the difference that matters for the app

Across the 79 action and movement-head labels:

| Accept only labels at confidence ≥ | Decide: share accepted, accuracy | Jev: share accepted, accuracy |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 100%, 83.5% | 100%, 92.4% |
| 0.5 | 56%, 93.2% | 100%, 92.4% |
| 0.6 | 23%, **100%** | 99%, 92.3% |
| 0.8 | 5%, 100% | 97%, 92.2% |

**Decide's confidence separates right from wrong: every error sat at 0.56 or below.** So in
the app it can propose everything, auto-accept above 0.6, and ask the user about the rest. Jev
is more accurate but confident when wrong, so a threshold cannot catch its errors. It needs the
person to review the whole proposal.

This is the opposite of b001 and b002. There, reading numbers, Decide's confidence was pinned
at 0.92 to 0.94 regardless of the case. Reading language, which is what it was built for, its
confidence is informative.

## Not claimed
