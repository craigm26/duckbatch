# r001 design: a plain-language router for the duck (pre-registered 2026-09-24)

Written, with `menus/r001-router-requests.yaml`, before any model saw the requests.

## Question

In b001 and b002, GLiNER2.5-Decide failed as a judge of numbers: it answered `kill`, gap 2 on all
17 cases. Its model card says its strength is operational language decisions (intents,
routing). So: can it turn "walk slowly while looking to the right" or "say hello then sit
down" into steps the duck can actually run? And can it refuse "open the fridge" instead of
forcing it onto a skill?

This router is the first piece of the Duck Studio plan editor Craig described (plain-language
requests proposing an editable flow chart; see `2026-09-24-ios-studio-integration.md`). It
**proposes** steps; a person edits and runs them.

## Method

- **Vocabulary:** the robot's own. The actions are walk forward or back, turn left or right,
  stop, the five duckkit skills (`kick_left`, `kick_right`, `sit_toggle`, `roulade`,
  `ground_pick`), a sound (the seven duckkit sounds), and `unsupported`. Speed is slow, normal
  or fast, and head is straight or look left, right, up or down. Each label carries a one-line
  description.
- **Splitting a request into clauses is code, not a model:** it splits on " then ",
  ", then", "; ", " and then " and " after that ". One model call per clause, with every head
  asked at once.
- **Mapping to the robot is code:** a step is a skill tag, a sound tag, or a twist within
  Pollen's command limits (forward at most 0.25 m/s, back 0.2, turn 1.0 rad/s), plus a head
  offset.
- **Models,** on identical clause text:
  - fastino/GLiNER2.5-Decide at `65624f1`, local, on CPU;
  - Jev (`jev-latest`), the same labels and descriptions as `choice` questions.
- **Metrics:**
  - per-head accuracy over the 55 gold clauses;
  - the share of whole requests where every step is exactly right;
  - on the 7 out-of-scope requests, how many are refused (`unsupported`) rather than mapped
    to a skill;
  - mean confidence of wrong answers.

## Expectation to beat

- **Decide:** action accuracy at least 80% on plain and paraphrased commands, 60 to 80% on
  head and speed, and it catches about half of the out-of-scope requests. Out-of-scope is its
  hardest case, because "climb the stairs" looks like walking.
- **Jev:** similar or better on actions, and better on out-of-scope.
- **Gate for the app:** the router may *propose* steps only if action accuracy is at least 85%
  and at least 5 of 7 out-of-scope requests are refused. Below that, it is not shown to users
  yet.

## Not claimed

The steps are a proposal and none of them has been run on a robot or in the sim here. Mimicry
from video or AR is a later, separate question.
