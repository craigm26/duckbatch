## Pass lines, as pre-registered
1. An arm with falls per 100 switches ≤ swapping's: **failed.** The best, m01, falls 1.6× as often.
2. That arm's walk gap ≤ 1.5× b002 a02's (≤ 0.0036): m01 (0.0021) and m02 (0.0028) **pass**; m03 fails.
3. No skill's gap > 3× its walk gap: **failed** for every arm, on the kicks alone (8× to 88×).

## What it means
- **Five skills fit in one small network.** Walk, stand, sit, unsit and pick are all reproduced
  at b002's walking precision by a network the teacher's size (m01), and nearly so at 58k
  parameters (m02). Capacity matters: m03 at 26k parameters is 2–4× worse everywhere.
- **The kicks did not distil.** A kick is a short, violent, open-loop motion, which a regression
  toward the teacher's action on the frames visited learns worst. At this budget the kicks are
  a gap of 0.02–0.19, and m01's right kick is the worst in the table.
- **Switching costs safety, for now.** In simulation, swapping Pollen's networks is already
  safe (about 1 fall per 100 switches), and one network is not yet safer. The design note
  predicted this might happen: the transitions need their own data, meaning more switches per
  episode and switches timed mid-skill, not just longer training.
- **Why it still matters for Duck Studio.** The app refuses any plan with a skill that isn't the
  last step, because a real skill network never hands control back. One network that holds
  every skill has no such limit, and b004 shows it's reachable for five of the seven skills.
